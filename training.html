01 / FIELD NOTES

Loss, gradients and generalization

Learning adjusts parameters to reduce an objective on examples; useful behavior must still transfer to new cases.

A forward pass predicts an output. A loss compares that output to a target or objective. Backpropagation computes gradients through the network; an optimizer updates parameters using a batch of examples. The learning rate controls the update scale. Adam-like optimizers use additional state, so their updates are more elaborate than the simple gradient step below.

Training loss measures fit to training examples. Development data supports model and hyperparameter choices; a held-out test assesses transfer after those choices are fixed. For a binary classifier, accuracy can hide class imbalance. For a language model, next-token loss alone does not measure source fidelity or tool-task success.

Simple gradient step: θₜ₊₁ = θₜ − η ∇θ Lbatch(θₜ) θ: learned parameters; η: learning rate

02 / FIELD NOTES

Pretraining and post-training do different work

An illustrative pipeline, not a disclosure of the internals of every commercial model.

Pretraining learns broad representations and prediction patterns from large corpora. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) uses demonstrations of desired behavior. Preference learning compares candidate outputs; an RLHF approach can fit a reward model and optimize a policy, while DPO optimizes a preference objective without that same explicit reward-model-plus-RL loop. These are families of methods; a provider may combine or replace them.

Better preference scores need not imply better factual accuracy, robust authorization or long-task completion. Define what behavior the training data rewards and what errors the evaluation still finds.

04 / FIELD NOTES

Data quality and evaluation determine the useful scale

Training loss, task quality and operational value are different measurements.

Deduplicate and version data; check provenance, licensing, privacy and contamination. Separate training, development and held-out evaluation before using examples to tune behavior. A model that memorizes answers to a test can look capable without transferring to new tasks.

Historical scaling-law studies examine relationships among parameters, data and compute within specific experimental regimes. They help plan experiments; they do not guarantee that any larger model is better at your workload. Track failure categories and the smallest system meeting your acceptance threshold.
